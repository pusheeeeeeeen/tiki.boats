The Tiki Fish
What we know about it so far
An artist's rendition of the tiki fish, based off of images and accounts.

The tiki fish is a rare fish, found in coral reefs. The tiki fish prefers warmer temperatures, and populations can usually not survive in temperatures below 80° Farenheit. The tiki fish has a very unique appearance: sparkling gold and green stripes, cyan translucent fins, and a very thin body. Some legends say that the fish’s stripes contain real gold, but as they are very rare, killing one to find out would go against the conservation policy, and no dead tiki fish have ever been found.

The tiki fish diet varies between the few locations that they are found, but they often eat seagrass and red algae, and rarely brain coral. Not only are tiki fish very rare, they are also extremely hard to spot. Although their colors do stand out, they have evolved to be extremely stealthy as a consequence of their small numbers, and almost never emerge when they see other moving organisms nearby. Because of this, only a few people have managed to get a clear view of a tiki fish, and only a handful of pictures have ever been taken.

Not much else is known about the tiki fish—because no dead ones have been found, it is likely that they have a long lifespan, but nobody can say for sure.

As more about the tiki fish is discovered, this page will be updated to contain the most up-to-date info possible.
